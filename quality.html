EDITORIAL STANDARD · VERSION 1.0

How resource quality is reviewed.

A transparent editorial rubric—not a promise that an AI model will always produce a correct result.

Scoring scale

Each dimension receives a whole-number score from 1 to 5.

  • 1 — Serious gap: not ready to publish.
  • 2 — Material weaknesses: substantial revision needed.
  • 3 — Adequate: usable, with identifiable improvements.
  • 4 — Strong: clear and useful with minor gaps.
  • 5 — Excellent: unusually clear, complete, and well-scoped.

Five review dimensions

  1. Clarity: Is the task understandable? Are instructions and placeholders unambiguous?
  2. Completeness: Does it include the context, constraints, steps, and output expectations needed?
  3. Usefulness: Would it help its intended audience complete a real, well-defined task?
  4. Originality: Is the wording and structure original and distinct, without copied third-party material?
  5. Limitations: Are uncertainty, assumptions, failure modes, and boundaries made clear?

How to interpret review status

  • Not assessed: no complete rubric assessment has been recorded.
  • Assessed: all five scores, reviewer, date, and notes are recorded.
  • Needs revision: at least one dimension scores 1 or 2, or a blocking issue is identified.
  • Ready: every dimension scores at least 3 and separate safety, rights, and technical checks pass.

Important distinctions

A quality score is not a model test, safety certification, factual guarantee, or formal accessibility audit. Model compatibility must have its own dated test record. Safety, privacy, accessibility, factual accuracy, and content rights are reviewed separately; a high score never overrides a blocking issue in those areas.

Current catalogue status

Existing catalogue entries are marked Not assessed until a human completes and records all five scores. No historical scores have been invented or backfilled. This makes the new standard visible without implying that reviews have already happened.
